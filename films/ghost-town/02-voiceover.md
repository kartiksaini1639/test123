# Ghost Town — Voiceover

About 2:10 of narration, 324 words, one narrator. Record it **before** you animate, because the real length of each line sets how long each clip runs.

## Delivery
- **Tone:** measured, warm, slightly wry, like a curious friend explaining something. Not a movie-trailer voice, not dramatic.
- **Pace:** about 150 words per minute. Slow down on the short punch lines ("Probably not." · "Make you love me.").
- **Pitch:** mid-low, conversational. Let sentence endings fall naturally rather than rise.
- **Song titles:** say *Ghost Town* and *Someday* lightly, with no extra emphasis.
- **Pronunciation:** Shirley Ann Lee = "SHUR-lee ann LEE". Kanye = "KAHN-yay". Genesis = "JEN-uh-sis".

## Pause markers
- `/` = short breath (about 0.3s)
- `//` = longer pause (about 0.8s)
- **Bold** = light stress

Most text-to-speech tools ignore these marks, so for TTS paste the **clean text** version further down instead. If you record yourself, read from the marked version.

## Cheapest ways to record
1. **Your own voice (free).** Use a phone voice-memo app in a quiet room with soft furnishings, such as a closet full of clothes. Hold the phone about 20cm from your mouth, slightly off to the side. Record each beat 2–3 times and keep the best take.
2. **A free tier of any text-to-speech tool.** Paste one beat at a time, keep the **same voice and settings** for all 16, and download each as its own file.

Name the files `vo_01.wav` to `vo_16.wav`. Leave about 0.3s of silence at the start and end of each.

## Clean text (for text-to-speech, one line per file)

```
01  The first voice you hear on Ghost Town isn't Kanye's. It belongs to a woman almost nobody has heard of.
02  Her name is Shirley Ann Lee. In the late nineteen-sixties, she records a short song called Someday. Then it sits on a shelf for forty years.
03  To understand why Kanye builds a song around her voice, you have to go back much further. To the Book of Revelation.
04  There, a woman appears crowned with twelve stars, in the middle of danger and turmoil. She suffers. And then she is delivered.
05  Ghost Town runs on the same idea. Suffering isn't the end of the story. It's the price of the release that comes after.
06  Now go back even further, to Genesis. God creates for six days. On the seventh, he rests.
07  Kanye seems to measure his own career against that cycle. Years of creating, nonstop. And someday, finally, a seventh day.
08  Listen to how often that word comes back. Someday. Someday. Someday. A song stuck waiting for something that still hasn't happened.
09  Every repetition carries three things at once. Hope. Tension. And frustration, because whatever he wants stays just out of reach.
10  Here's where it gets darker. In the song, freedom looks like getting high and doing whatever he wants.
11  But if you have to escape constantly just to feel free, are you actually free? Probably not.
12  That contradiction is the point. The way Kanye chases freedom is the very thing keeping him from it.
13  Read it that way, and the song becomes Kanye talking to himself. Every escape pulls him further from who he is.
14  Which makes one line hit differently. Make you love me.
15  Kanye splits into two people. One, desperate to be loved. The other, the one whose love he's trying to earn. Self-love has become so foreign, it feels like courting a stranger.
16  Shirley Ann Lee's someday came forty years late. Kanye's is still out there. Waiting for him to arrive.
```

## After recording
- Write down the real length of each file. If one runs more than about 2s over its estimate, trim words in that beat rather than speeding the voice up.
- Make the matching clip for each shot **as long as its voiceover plus 0.5s**.
- Keep peaks around -6 dB and remove background hum with your editor's noise-reduction filter.
