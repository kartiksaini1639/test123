# Ghost Town — Background Music

One instrumental bed for the whole film, about 2:20 long. **No vocals**, because they fight the narration. **Don't use the actual song or the Shirley Ann Lee sample**, because that can get the video muted or taken down for copyright.

## Overall brief
- **Mood:** haunted, contemplative, aching but hopeful. Empty-desert loneliness that slowly warms.
- **Genre texture:** sparse cinematic underscore with a lo-fi soul feel. Think dusty 1960s soul record played in an empty room.
- **Tempo:** 72–78 BPM, slow and steady.
- **Key:** minor (A minor or D minor), lifting toward the relative major at the end.
- **Instruments:** warm felt piano, soft Rhodes or organ pad, brushed drums or a muted kick, faint vinyl crackle, a distant lonely slide guitar or whistle (the "ghost town" texture), and low strings in the second half.
- **Avoid:** vocals, choirs, big drops, EDM builds, busy hi-hats, anything louder or brighter than the narration.

## Prompt for any AI music generator
Paste this into any text-to-music tool, with "instrumental" turned on if it has that option:

> Instrumental only, no vocals. Haunted, contemplative cinematic underscore with a dusty 1960s soul feel, 75 BPM, A minor. Warm felt piano and soft Rhodes organ pad over faint vinyl crackle, brushed drums entering gently, a distant lonely slide guitar like wind through an empty desert ghost town. Starts sparse and lonely, builds slowly with low strings into tense, unresolved longing, drops to near-silence for a moment, then resolves warmly and hopefully into a major chord at the end. Documentary background music, sits under narration, no sudden drops, no big percussion.

If the tool caps track length, generate two takes of about 1:10 each with the same prompt. Use one take for 0:00–1:20 and the other for 1:20–2:20, cross-fading over 2s at the cut.

## Mixing tips
- The voiceover is always the loudest thing. Set the music about 15–18 dB below the voice while someone is speaking.
- Most editors can automatically lower the music while the voice plays. This is called "ducking" or "auto volume". If yours can't, draw the volume line by hand.
- Fade the music in over 2s at the start and out over 3s at the end.
- Export the final mix at 48 kHz, peaking no higher than -1 dB.
